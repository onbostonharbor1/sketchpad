Undo in Figures: a Proposal

Written 27 October 2026 for discussion. Nothing here is built. Barry asked where undo came from: it came up while specifying the next weld pass (an accidental handle drop would weld, and there is no way back except right-click). Barry also recalls an earlier remark that undo could be done by taking snapshots of a portion of uiState. This page works that idea out.

The idea in one paragraph

Everything that makes a figure what it is lives in one plain-data object, uiState.figures.tabs[tabId].figure: its layers (each with its object's parameters, color, visibility and transform), its discs, its welds, the stacking order, the active layer. Plain data can be copied whole with structuredClone(). So: before each change, push a copy onto a stack. Undo pops the copy, puts it back, and rebuilds the live drawing objects from it. Nothing has to know how to reverse its own change; there is no list of inverse operations to keep correct.

Why it is cheap here

  • A figure is small: a few kilobytes even with dozens of layers and discs. Fifty copies is nothing.
  • It was kept plain data on purpose (see figureModel.js), for Save As. Undo gets the same benefit.
  • Welds, discs and layers are all in the one object, so a snapshot can never restore a weld without the discs it refers to.

The one real piece of work: rebuilding the instances

Each layer has a live instance (a private copy of its registry object) in tabState.instances, and today layer.params and instance.params are the same object. After a snapshot is put back, those links are broken: the restored layer.params is a copy. So every instance has to be made again from layer.id and layer.params, with its callbacks (redrawHandler, getTransform, yieldTo, beforeMove, afterMove) attached.

insertLayer() already does exactly this for one layer, mixed in with "add a new layer". The proposal is to pull out a makeInstance(tabId, index) that both use. Phase 10 (reopening a saved figure) needs the same function, which is the strongest reason to build undo next to it: one rebuild path, written once, tested twice.

When to take a snapshot

Once per action, before it starts, never on every mouse move:

ActionSnapshot taken
Drag a handleon the press, not each move (the first beforeMove of a drag)
Move or turn a layeron the press on the diamond or the rotation handle
Arrow-key nudgeonce per run of presses (a pause starts a new step)
Insert, Break apartbefore
Place, delete or drag a discbefore
Weld, unweldbefore
Show or hide a layer, change its colorbefore
Move a sliderwhen the slider is let go (its change event), not while it slides

A new action after an undo clears the redo stack, as usual.

What is not undone

View state, which is not part of the figure: Points Mode, Show All, the selected disc or handle, whether the centroid is selected. Undo leaves those as they are (and clears the selections, since the thing selected may be gone).

How it would look

  • Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y (and Ctrl+Shift+Z) redo, when the keyboard is not in a text box or slider.
  • Undo and Redo in the caption menu, grayed when there is nothing to undo or redo.
  • One stack per figure subtab, kept in its tabState (view state, not saved with the figure).

Where the code would go

  • A new ui/figures/figureUndo.js: snapshot(tabId), undo(tabId), redo(tabId), the stacks and their limit.
  • figureLayers.js: makeInstance(), and a rebuildInstances(tabId) that undo and Phase 10 call.
  • One snapshot() call at the start of each action listed above (about a dozen places).

Questions for Barry

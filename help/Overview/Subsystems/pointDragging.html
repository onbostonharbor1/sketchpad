Point Dragging

Written 27 October 2026. Sketchpad has four things you can drag, handled by three different files. This page puts them side by side, says which one wins when they overlap, and follows a drag through to the redraw.

The four draggable things

An anchored disc is not draggable. It sits at a fixed fraction along its object's infrastructure and moves when the object does.

Who gets the press

All three files listen to the mouse at once. In order of precedence:

  1. The centroid and its rotation handle (lines mode only). The interactor asks yieldTo(x, y) first and stays out if the centroid wants the press. A Line's midpoint handle sits exactly under its centroid, so on a Line the centroid always wins there.
  2. A handle of the active object, within 10 px.
  3. In points mode, a free disc under the press (within the larger of the hit radius and the disc's 9 px).
  4. Otherwise, in points mode, the press is a click (place or select a disc).

Only the left button drags. Right-click in points mode is the weld toggle and drags nothing. (The centroid's handler does not yet check the button; a right-press there starts a move. Known, listed in PHASES.md.)

Click or drag?

  • Handles: any movement drags. The handle is placed exactly under the pointer.
  • Points mode: a press on a handle that moves more than 4 px is the handle being dragged; a press that stays within 4 px is a click (so a disc can be placed on a handle). A press anywhere else is always a click where it went down, even if the hand slips, because dragging empty canvas means nothing.

Arrow keys

  • With a handle selected: the handle moves 1 px, 10 with Shift (Draw and Figures).
  • With the centroid selected: the whole layer moves 1 px, 10 with Shift.
  • Keys are ignored while typing in a text box, slider or list.

Object space and canvas space

In Figures every layer has a transform (move and turn). Handles and free discs are stored in the layer's own coordinates ("object space") and shown, hit and dragged in canvas pixels. Every drag converts the pointer position back through the transform before storing it, so a sideways drag moves the handle sideways on screen even on a layer that has been turned. The math is in draw/drawTransform.js.

A handle drag, start to finish (Figures)

  1. interactor.js grabs the handle on the press.
  2. Each mouse move calls moveHandle():
    1. beforeMove() snapshots where this layer's welded discs are.
    2. The pointer position, in object space, goes into params.points[i].
    3. The object's update() rebuilds its geometry; redrawFigure() redraws every layer and moves every anchored disc to its fraction along its arm.
    4. afterMove() compares with the snapshot. Each welded disc that moved passes the same move to its partners on other layers: one handle, both ends of an arm, or the whole layer, depending on where the partner sits (figureWelds.js).
  3. Release ends the drag.

A centroid move or turn goes the same way, through setTransform() in figureCentroid.js, with the same snapshot-and-follow around it.

What a midpoint handle does

A Line and a Parabola keep a midpoint in their handle list. The object's update() notices that the midpoint moved and slides the whole line instead of stretching it. So dragging a line's middle moves the line; dragging an end reshapes it.
